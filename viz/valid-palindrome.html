left starts at the front, right at the back. Each one first skips anything that isn't a letter or digit, then the two characters are compared ignoring case. A match means both step inward. One mismatch and it's not a palindrome. No cleaned copy of the string is ever built.

Why left < right inside the skip loops too: without it, a string of only punctuation (like ".,") would let left run past the end. It also stops the two pointers from crossing while skipping.

  
Why tolower on both sides: 'A' and 'a' are different chars ('A' = 65, 'a' = 97). Lower-casing both makes the comparison ignore case. Digits are unchanged by tolower.

  
Try "0P": both are letters or digits, but '0' ≠ 'p', so it's false. A check that only looked at letters would get this wrong.
