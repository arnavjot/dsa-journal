Alien Dictionary, step by step (Kahn's BFS)

Compare each word with the next one. The first letter that differs gives one rule: that letter of the first word comes before that letter of the second. Those rules form a graph of letters; a topological sort of it is the alphabet. Kahn's algorithm keeps taking a letter with indegree 0 (nothing left that must come before it). Two traps: a longer word before its own prefix ("abc" then "ab") is impossible, and letters left over at the end are stuck in a cycle.

Why only the first differing letter: once two words differ at position j, the rest of the letters never affect how they sort.
Why "abc" before "ab" is impossible: a word always sorts after its own prefix, whatever the alphabet.
Why leftovers mean a cycle: letters on a cycle each wait for another one on it, so none of them reaches indegree 0.
Cost: O(C) to read all C characters, plus O(26²) for the letter graph, which is constant.
