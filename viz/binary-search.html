Binary Search, step by step

The array is sorted, so one look at the middle tells you which half the target can't be in. Throw that half away and repeat. The search space halves every step: 16 numbers take at most 5 looks, a million take about 20.

Why s + (e − s) / 2 and not (s + e) / 2: same middle, but s + e can overflow an int when both are near 2³¹. e − s never does.

  
Why mid + 1 and mid − 1: mid was just checked and isn't the target, so it's thrown away too. Using s = mid could loop forever when s and e are next to each other.

  
Why s <= e (not <): when s == e there's still one number left to check. The loop only stops once s passes e, meaning nothing is left: return −1.
