Largest Rectangle in Histogram, step by step

The stack keeps bar indices with rising heights. When a shorter bar i arrives, every taller bar on top can't stretch any further right, so it's popped and measured: its height × the width between the new stack top and i − 1. At the end, whatever is left stretches all the way to the right edge.

Why the popped bar's rectangle ends at i − 1: bar i is shorter, so the rectangle can't go through it. It's the first shorter bar to the right.

  
Why it starts after the new stack.top(): the stack is rising, so the bar below the popped one is the nearest shorter (or equal) bar to the left. Empty stack means nothing shorter on the left, so the width is all of 0 … i − 1, which is i.

  
Why the final loop with i = n: bars still on the stack never met a shorter bar on the right, so they stretch to the end. Using i = n is like adding a height-0 bar after the last one.
