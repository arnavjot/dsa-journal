Two Sum II, step by step

The array is sorted, so the sum of the two ends tells you which pointer to move. Too small → the left number is too small for any partner still in range, so move left right. Too big → the right number is too big for any partner, so move right left. One pass, no extra memory.

Why moving a pointer never skips the answer: if the sum is too small, numbers[left] plus the biggest number still in range is too small, so numbers[left] can't pair with anything left. It's safe to drop it. Too big works the same way for numbers[right].

  
Why left + 1, right + 1: this problem numbers the array from 1, not 0. The small grey numbers under each box show that.

  
Why not the hash map from Two Sum I: it works, but uses O(n) memory. The problem asks for constant extra space, and sorting makes the two pointers possible.
